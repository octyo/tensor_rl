The first thing I would investigate is whether the CP model is simply too constrained for the layers you are compressing. In tensor_layers.py, your CP layer is a low-rank factorization of each dense matrix, and in q_networks.py you use the same `rank` for both hidden layers and the final action head. With `hidden_sizes=(128, 128)` and `rank=4`, the effective rank bottleneck is very strong, so the CP model may be underfitting even if the DQN loop is fine.

The second area is optimization, not architecture. Factorized layers often train worse than dense layers even when they are mathematically valid, because the factors are scale-indeterminate and can become ill-conditioned. I would inspect:
- the norm of each factor matrix separately
- the effective weight matrix rank or singular values during training
- whether one factor grows while the other shrinks
- sensitivity to the same Adam learning rate used for the dense model

Also check initialization. Your CP factors use the same uniform scheme as a normal linear layer. That is usually not a neutral choice for a product of factors, because the product changes the effective variance of the layer. A CP model can look “dead” early if its initial effective weights are too small, or unstable if they are too large.

A very practical issue in your current setup is observability. The logger in logging_utils.py only stores episode reward, length, and time, and train.py does not record the `loss` returned by `DQNAgent.update()` from dqn_agent.py. That means you currently cannot tell whether the CP model is failing because of:
- slow optimization
- unstable TD targets
- under-capacity
- or simply slower convergence

For debugging, I would add temporary logging for:
- TD loss
- mean and max Q-values
- mean target Q-values
- TD-error histogram or at least mean absolute TD error
- gradient norms for the CP factors
- action entropy or action frequency

When you look at training behavior, these patterns are the most informative:
- Loss decreases, reward does not: likely undercapacity or poor representation
- Loss is noisy or spikes repeatedly: likely optimization instability from the factorized parameterization
- Q-values collapse to a narrow range: likely too much compression or vanishing effective weights
- Q-values explode while reward stays flat: likely bootstrapping instability
- One action dominates too early: possibly bad initialization or overconfident value estimates
- Standard DQN learns faster on the same number of environment steps: maybe the CP model needs more steps before it can compete, so compare by steps, not only by episode count

Common pitfalls for tensor-network-style DQNs are:
- rank too low for the hidden widths
- applying the same rank to every layer, especially the output layer
- using a dense-model learning rate unchanged
- flattening observations so the tensor structure gives no real inductive benefit
- expecting compression to help when the task is already small enough for a normal MLP
- evaluating only reward curves and not the internal Q/TD statistics

So the shortest debugging path is: compare parameter counts, sweep rank, log loss/Q-statistics, and inspect factor norms and singular values. That will tell you whether the gap is mainly capacity, initialization, or optimization.
